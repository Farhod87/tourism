A Brief History of Samarkand

Samarkand is one of the oldest cities in the world, founded over 2750 years ago. The city was a key center on the Great Silk Road and the capital of Tamerlane's empire.

Great scholars, astronomers, and poets created here. Mirzo Ulugh Beg built the famous observatory, and Amir Timur erected architectural masterpieces that still amaze the world. Today, Samarkand is the pearl of Uzbekistan and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Spiritual Shrines of Samarkand

Great Sufi mentors and places of pilgrimage that illuminate the spiritual history of the city.

01

Imam Al-Bukhari

A great hadith scholar, the "Sultan of Muhaddiths". His memorial complex near Samarkand is one of the main places of pilgrimage in the Islamic world.

02

Makhdumi Azam

An outstanding Sufi scholar, one of the leading mentors of the Naqshbandi order. Author of over 30 works. His mausoleum in Dakhbed is an important center of pilgrimage.

03

Khoja Akhrar Vali

An outstanding religious thinker of the 15th century, the head of the Naqshbandi Sufi order. He influenced the spiritual and political life of the Timurid era.

04

Sheikh Nuriddin Basir

A Sufi sheikh of the 13th century who reached the highest spiritual degree of "Kutbi-Chaardahum". Amir Timur revered him and often visited his mausoleum before campaigns.

05

Burkhaneddin Sagarji

A spiritual mentor, the head of the Muslims of Beijing. According to his will, he was buried next to the mausoleum of Nuriddin Basir. The Rukhabad mausoleum was erected over his grave.

06

Sheikh Shamsiddin Kulal

The spiritual mentor of the young Amir Timur. He had a profound influence on his religious views and inspired him to a great destiny.

07

Sheikh Zayniddin Kuyi Orifon

A famous Tashkent sheikh, the mentor of Nuriddin Basir. The Basir mausoleum was built by order of Amir Timur, who also erected a mausoleum over the grave of this sheikh in Tashkent.
